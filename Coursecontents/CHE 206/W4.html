Topic 4.1
Deriving the Four Property Relations

Everything starts from two places: the First Law (ΔU = Q − W) and the Second Law (dS = dQrev/T). Combine them and you get the master equation for internal energy. Then define three more thermodynamic functions — enthalpy H, Helmholtz energy A, and Gibbs energy G — and differentiate each to get the four fundamental relations.

Step-by-step derivation
Start — Combine First and Second Laws

From 1st Law: dU = dQ − PdV. From 2nd Law (reversible): dQ = TdS.
Therefore: dU = TdS − PdV

Enthalpy H = U + PV

dH = dU + PdV + VdP = TdS − PdV + PdV + VdP → dH = TdS + VdP

Helmholtz Energy A = U − TS

dA = dU − TdS − SdT = TdS − PdV − TdS − SdT → dA = −PdV − SdT

Gibbs Energy G = H − TS

dG = dH − TdS − SdT = TdS + VdP − TdS − SdT → dG = VdP − SdT

Summary — four fundamental relations
dU = TdS − PdV
dH = TdS + VdP
dA = −PdV − SdT
dG = VdP − SdT
Exam-ready — what each function represents
Internal energy U: total energy stored in a system (kinetic + potential of molecules)
Enthalpy H = U + PV: useful for constant-pressure processes — heat added at constant P equals ΔH
Helmholtz energy A = U − TS: maximum work obtainable from a system at constant T and V
Gibbs energy G = H − TS: maximum non-expansion work at constant T and P; criterion for phase and chemical equilibrium
Common mistake
When deriving, students often substitute the wrong sign. dW = PdV for work done BY the system. If work is done ON the system, the sign flips. Track signs carefully at each step. Also: these relations assume a reversible process.
