Topic 6.1
The Second Law — Two Statements & Entropy

The Second Law comes in two classic statements that say the same thing from different angles. Both were derived from observations about heat engines.

Two statements of the Second Law
Kelvin-Planck Statement

No apparatus can operate in such a way that its only effect is to convert heat absorbed by a system completely into work done by the system. It is impossible by a cyclic process to convert heat completely into work.

Clausius Statement

No process is possible which consists solely in the transfer of heat from one temperature level to a higher one. Heat cannot spontaneously flow from cold to hot.

Entropy change calculation
ΔS = Q/T   (reversible heat transfer at constant T)
ΔS_total = Q/T_cold − Q/T_hot
Spontaneous processes: ΔS_total > 0  ·  Reversible processes: ΔS_total = 0
Worked example — entropy change (past exam)
Given: Q = 120 kJ transferred from 650K to 280K

ΔS_hot = −120/650 = −0.1846 kJ/K  (loses heat → negative)
ΔS_cold = +120/280 = +0.4286 kJ/K  (gains heat → positive)
ΔS_total = −0.1846 + 0.4286 = +0.244 kJ/K
Positive → irreversible, spontaneous process ✓

Exam-ready definitions
The Second Law (Kelvin-Planck): It is impossible by a cyclic process to convert the heat absorbed by a system completely into work done by the system.

The Second Law (Clausius): No process is possible which consists solely in the transfer of heat from one temperature level to a higher one.

Entropy: A thermodynamic property that measures the degree of disorder or unavailability of energy in a system. For a reversible process at temperature T: dS = dQrev/T.
