Topic 10.1
Dryness Fraction & Wet Vapour Properties

In the two-phase region, you have a mixture of saturated liquid and saturated vapour. The dryness fraction x (also called quality) tells you the fraction of the total mass that is vapour. x = 0 means all liquid. x = 1 means all vapour (dry saturated). Anything between is wet vapour.

Dryness fraction and specific properties of wet vapour
x = mg / (mg + mf) = mg / mmix
ṽ = ṽf + x · (ṽg − ṽf) = ṽf + x · ṽfg
ũ = ũf + x · ũfg     ĥ = ĥf + x · ĥfg     s̃ = s̃f + x · s̃fg
mg = mass of vapour, mf = mass of liquid, x = dryness fraction (0 ≤ x ≤ 1)
Subscript f = saturated liquid, g = saturated vapour, fg = difference (g minus f)
Worked example — pressure cooker (2022/23 past question)
Given: V = 0.20 m³, P = 4 bar, m = 0.65 kg
Step 1 — Specific volume of mixture

ṽ = V/m = 0.20/0.65 = 0.3077 m³/kg

Step 2 — Read steam table at P = 4 bar

ṽf = 0.001084 m³/kg, ṽg = 0.4625 m³/kg

Step 3 — Find quality x

ṽ = ṽf + x(ṽg − ṽf) → 0.3077 = 0.001084 + x(0.4625 − 0.001084)
x = 0.3066/0.4614 = 0.665 (66.5% vapour)

Step 4 — Mass of liquid

mf = (1 − x) × m = (1 − 0.665) × 0.65 = 0.218 kg

Exam-ready definition
The dryness fraction (quality) x of a wet vapour is defined as the mass of dry saturated vapour divided by the total mass of the wet mixture. x = mg/(mg + mf). For saturated liquid x = 0, for dry saturated vapour x = 1. The specific volume of a wet vapour is: ṽ = ṽf + x·ṽfg, where ṽfg = ṽg − ṽf.
Common mistake
Using x instead of (1−x) for the liquid fraction. x is the vapour fraction. The liquid fraction is (1−x). Mass of liquid = (1−x) × total mass. Students regularly swap these under time pressure.
