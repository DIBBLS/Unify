Week 11 of 12 Clapeyron Equation — Latent Heat from Phase Change

The Clapeyron equation connects vapour pressure, temperature, and latent heat of vaporisation. It lets you calculate latent heat from measurable vapour-pressure data — without a calorimeter. The benzene-style calculation is a regular exam question and the vapour pressure equation version appears in CHE 206 specifically.

Topic 11.1
Deriving and Applying the Clapeyron Equation

The key insight that makes the Clapeyron equation possible is this: for a pure substance, the molar Gibbs energy does not change during a phase transition. At equilibrium, Gα = Gβ (Gibbs energy is the same for both phases). This gives us a constraint that leads directly to the equation.

The Clapeyron equation
dPsat/dT = ΔH / (T · ΔV)
ΔH = latent heat of phase change (J/kg)
ΔV = volume change = Vg − Vf (m³/kg)
T = absolute temperature (K)
Psat = saturation (vapour) pressure
Using a vapour pressure equation — CHE 206 method
Given: ln(Psat/Pa) = 20.9042 − 3456.8/T, at T = 378K
Step 1 — Find dPsat/dT

d[ln(Psat)]/dT = 3456.8/T²
dPsat/dT = Psat × 3456.8/T²

Step 2 — Calculate Psat at T = 378K

ln(Psat/Pa) = 20.9042 − 3456.8/378 = 11.7593
Psat = e^11.7593 = 127,600 Pa

Step 3 — Calculate dPsat/dT at T = 378K

dPsat/dT = 127,600 × 3456.8/378² = 3084 Pa/K

Step 4 — Apply Clapeyron equation

ΔV = vg − vf = 2.541 − 0.002 = 2.539 m³/kg
ΔH = T × ΔV × dPsat/dT = 378 × 2.539 × 3084 = 2,960 kJ/kg

Exam-ready — what made the derivation possible
The derivation of the Clapeyron equation is made possible by the fact that for a pure substance at phase equilibrium, the molar Gibbs energy is the same for both phases (Gα = Gβ). Since Gibbs energy does not change during a phase transition at constant T and P, the differential changes in Gibbs energy for both phases must be equal, leading directly to: dPsat/dT = ΔH/(TΔV).
Common mistake
Forgetting to subtract vf from vg when calculating ΔV. For liquid-vapour: ΔV = vg − vf. Since vg >> vf, this is approximately just vg — but in exam problems where vf is given, you must subtract it. Also: temperatures must be in Kelvin.
