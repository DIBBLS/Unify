Week 9 of 12 Heat Pumps, Refrigerators & Coefficient of Performance

A refrigerator is a heat engine running backwards. Instead of converting heat to work, you put work in to move heat from cold to hot. The measure of how well this works is COP, not efficiency. The cold storage design problem is a classic exam calculation.

Topic 9.1
Refrigerators, Heat Pumps & COP

A heat pump or refrigerator runs a thermodynamic cycle in reverse. Work W is added to the system, which absorbs heat QC from a cold reservoir and rejects heat QH to a hot reservoir. The first law still holds: QH = QC + W.

The same device is called a refrigerator if its goal is to extract heat from a cold body, and a heat pump if its goal is to deliver heat to a warm body. Same physics, different objective.

COP for refrigerator and heat pump
COP_refrigerator = ω = QC/W = TC/(TH − TC)
COP_heat_pump = ω = QH/W = TH/(TH − TC)
COP can be greater than 1 (unlike efficiency which is always less than 1)
QH = QC + W (first law for the cycle)
Worked example — cold storage (CHE 206 past question)
Given: T_cold = −25°C = 248K, T_ambient = 35°C = 308K, heat leaking in = 5 kJ/s
Step 1 — Maximum COP (Carnot)

COP = TC/(TH − TC) = 248/(308 − 248) = 248/60 = 4.133

Step 2 — Minimum work input

W = QC/COP = 5/4.133 = 1.21 kW. This is the minimum — any real refrigerator needs more.

Analogy
Think of a refrigerator like pushing a boulder uphill. Heat naturally flows downhill (hot to cold). A refrigerator pushes it uphill — but you have to do work to make it happen. The COP tells you how efficient that uphill push is. A COP of 4 means you move 4 kW of heat for every 1 kW of work — very efficient.
Exam-ready definitions
A heat pump or refrigerator is a device which works in a cycle, absorbing energy as heat from a low-temperature reservoir and rejecting energy as heat to a high-temperature reservoir, when work is done on the device.

COP is defined as the ratio of the energy effect sought to the work done on the device. For a refrigerator: COP = QC/W = TC/(TH − TC). For a heat pump: COP = QH/W = TH/(TH − TC).
Common mistake
Using the efficiency formula (η = 1 − TC/TH) for a refrigerator. Wrong — refrigerators use COP, not efficiency. Also: COP can be greater than 1. That's fine — you're moving more heat than you're putting in as work, not creating energy.
