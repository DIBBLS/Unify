Week 8 of 12 Steam Power Plant & The Rankine Cycle

The Rankine cycle is the practical replacement for the Carnot cycle in real steam power plants. It solves the engineering problems that make the Carnot cycle impractical with wet steam. Drawing and explaining the Rankine cycle on a T-S diagram is a guaranteed exam question.

Topic 8.1
Why Not Carnot? The Case for Rankine

The Carnot cycle is theoretically perfect but practically a nightmare with steam. The problem is step 2→3 (adiabatic expansion of saturated vapour): it produces a wet mixture of liquid and vapour that causes severe erosion of turbine blades. And step 4→1 requires a pump that handles a liquid-vapour mixture, which is mechanically very difficult to design.

The Rankine cycle fixes this by condensing the steam fully to saturated liquid first, then pumping the liquid. This avoids the erosion problem and the problematic pump design.

The four Rankine cycle steps
1 → 2: Constant-pressure heating in the boiler

Liquid water is heated at constant pressure. Becomes saturated vapour (and superheated if a superheater is added).

2 → 3: Reversible adiabatic (isentropic) expansion in turbine

Steam expands and does work. Temperature and pressure drop.

3 → 4: Constant-pressure, constant-temperature condensation in condenser

Wet steam fully condenses to saturated liquid. Heat QC is rejected to cooling water.

4 → 1: Reversible adiabatic (isentropic) pumping back to boiler pressure

Saturated liquid is pumped to high pressure. Produces subcooled liquid. Work input required.

Exam-ready definition
A steam power plant is a simple steady-state, steady-flow process in which steam generated in a boiler is expanded in an adiabatic turbine to produce work. The discharge stream from the turbine passes to a condenser from which it is pumped adiabatically back to the boiler. The net power output equals |QH| minus |QC|.
Common mistake
Drawing the Rankine cycle as a rectangle on the T-S diagram. It's not a rectangle — the boiler step follows the saturation curve. The pump step (4→1) is nearly vertical and the condenser step (3→4) is horizontal. Get the shape right.
