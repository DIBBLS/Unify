Week 5 of 12 Maxwell's Equations — Criterion of Exactness

Maxwell's equations let you swap unmeasurable quantities (like entropy changes) for measurable ones (like pressure and volume changes). They're derived mechanically from the four property relations using one simple mathematical rule. This appears in both derivation and proof questions.

Topic 5.1
Criterion of Exactness & Deriving Maxwell's Equations

The criterion of exactness is a mathematical rule: if F = f(x,y) and dF = Mdx + Ndy, then for the expression to be an exact differential (path-independent), the following must hold:

Criterion of exactness
(∂M/∂y)ₓ = (∂N/∂x)y
This works because mixed second partial derivatives are equal: ∂²F/∂y∂x = ∂²F/∂x∂y
The four Maxwell equations — full derivation
From dU = TdS − PdV → M=T, x=S, N=−P, y=V

(∂T/∂V)s = −(∂P/∂S)v

From dH = TdS + VdP → M=T, x=S, N=V, y=P

(∂T/∂P)s = (∂V/∂S)P

From dA = −PdV − SdT → M=−P, x=V, N=−S, y=T

(∂P/∂T)v = (∂S/∂V)T

From dG = VdP − SdT → M=V, x=P, N=−S, y=T

(∂V/∂T)P = −(∂S/∂P)T

Proving (∂H/∂P)T = V − T(∂V/∂T)P — past exam proof
From dH = TdS + VdP, divide by dP at constant T

(∂H/∂P)T = T(∂S/∂P)T + V

Apply Maxwell from dG: (∂S/∂P)T = −(∂V/∂T)P

(∂H/∂P)T = T × [−(∂V/∂T)P] + V

Final result

(∂H/∂P)T = V − T(∂V/∂T)P ✓

Exam-ready — Maxwell's equations (all four)
(∂T/∂V)s = −(∂P/∂S)v — from dU = TdS − PdV
(∂T/∂P)s = (∂V/∂S)P — from dH = TdS + VdP
(∂P/∂T)v = (∂S/∂V)T — from dA = −PdV − SdT
(∂V/∂T)P = −(∂S/∂P)T — from dG = VdP − SdT
Common mistake
Getting the signs wrong. The negative signs in Maxwell's equations come directly from the signs in the property relations. For dA = −PdV − SdT: (∂(−P)/∂T)v = (∂(−S)/∂V)T → (∂P/∂T)v = (∂S/∂V)T — the negatives cancel. Trace every sign.
