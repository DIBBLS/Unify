Topic 2.9
Coal Reserves and Production

The latest estimated coal reserves in India are placed at around 221 billions Tones. The provide indicated and inferred reserves are projected as 8499 and 38 billion tons respectively.

Table 2.6 reveals that out of the proven of 84 billions tons 81% or nearly 68 billion tons are of non-coking while remaining 16 billions tons are only coking coal.

Currently, the lignite reserves have been estimated at around 35 billions tons in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Kerala, Tamil Naidu, Jammu and Kashmir and Pondicherry. The characteristics of Indian coals are high ash content, low heating value and low sulphur content. Total 314 millions Tons of coal produced nearly 54% is consumed by railways, 23% by merry-Go-Round (MGR) systems and remaining by road and thermal power stations.

Topic 2.9.1
Sustainable Development Through Coal Gasification

For this purpose CSIR has recommended three prolonged approach:

  1. Immediate: The immediate targets are to establish coal gasification and IGCC technologies for commercial application.
  2. Short Term: (a) To construct commercial scale IGCC plants of 250 mW capacity. (b) The conversion of existing thermal power plants to IGCC. (c) To synthise coal gas for production of chemicals, steel and fertilizers.
  3. Long Term: (i) Repacing natural gas with coal gas in the existing combined cycle power plants. (ii) Self Relience and security in the energy sector. (iii) Development of advanced technologies based on coal gas. (iv) Establishment of plants for coal to oil and coal to refinery in commercial scale. (v) Technology development for fuel cell. (vi) Substitution of exhaustible with renewable energy sources.

Topic 2.10
Nuclear Energy Scenario

The nuclear power program is based on Natural Uranium in the first stage and subsequent development to replace Uranium with Thorium in the second stage. The Uranium reserve can support a program of 10⁴ MWe power generation based on pressurised heavy water reactors (PHWR) technology. Fast breeder reactors (FBR) technology for power generation is under development. FBR technology would be fuelled by Plutorium recovered and reprocessed from the spent fuel of PHWR program. At present India has 14 operating nuclear power reactor 2 boiling water reactor (BWR) and remaing PHWR. It has total installed capacity of 2.72 MWe.

Three Stages of Nuclear Programme:

  1. Stage 1: PHWR using natural Uranium (0.77% U²³⁵ and 99.23% U²³⁸) as fuel and heavy water both as coolant and moderator. A PHWR is a thermal reactor which consumes only U²³⁵ part of natural Uranium and U²³⁸ is left as spent fuel.
  2. Stage 2: Construction of FBR, where the spent fuel U²³⁸ is converted into Plutonium (fissionable material), which is then used as a fuel in a thermal reactor.
  3. Stage 3: Thorium ²³² is converted to U²³³ in a fast breeder reactor (FBR). The energy generated in the second stage was 60 times more than that available from PHWR for the same amount of Uranium.

Topic 2.11
Natural Gas Scenario

Before large scale discoveries, the estimated reserves of the natural gas was 680 billion cubic meters (BCM). Nearly 60% of this reserves are in western offshore and remaing balance is spread over Gujarat, North East India and South region.

The ministry of pollution and natural gas (MOPNG) made three type of allocation based on the recommendation of the gas linkage committee (GLC):

  1. Firm Allocation: Made to consumers where the availability of gas is assured by the producers and consumers are fairly established. The contract would generally be subjected to an 80% consumed and/or pay arrangement requiring a minimum offtake or pay penalties.
  2. Fall Back Allocation: Made to consumers when availability of gas for supply is dependent on failure of consumers with firm allocation to draw the allocated gas.
  3. Principle Allocation: Made to consumers based on the projected availability of gas. In light of excess of the projected demand over production no in principle allocation are being made currently.

The nature gas has found much use in fertilizer and power sectors. Their requirement are nearly 35% and 38% respectively. The demand of natural gas has been estimated as 593 MMSCMD by 2019-20.
