Energy Consumption as a Measure of Prosperity

Energy plays important role in judging the prosperity of a country. Standard of living of a given country is directly related to per capita energy consumption. Per capita sales of petroleum products in major states of India (in Kg) are, depicted in Table: 2.1. It is well know that per capita sales of petroleum produce on average basis is 126.6 Kg. These are classified in three categories.

  1. Above the national level
  2. Near the national level
  3. Below the national level

The main reasons for energy crisis is due to the two reason; Firstly- Enhancement of world population and secondly- increase in the standard of living or more consciousness towards comfort. If the annular per capita income of various countries are ploted against per capita energy consumption: It appears that the per capita energy consumption is a measure of the prosperity of the countries/states. Per capita energy consumption of U.S.A is 50 times than India and so is the prosperity of the country. The per capita consumption of energy in U.S.A is 12,000 kwh/year whereas the same value for India is 240 kwh/year. U.S.A with 9% of world population consumes, 40% of the total consumed in the world. Whereas for India with 25% of world population consumes hardly 2% of the total energy consumed world wide. If the standard of living in the developing countries is to be improved to match with the developed countries, the energy requirement the world will be much more than estimated above.

Topic 2.4
Alternate Energy Strategies

These strategies have been made due to:

  1. Supply of oil will not meet the increasing demand of enhanced population.
  2. Oil production will hasten this shortage and thereby reducing the time available for action on alternatives.
  3. Nature gas reserves are large enough to sustain the projected demand provided the incentives are sufficient to encourage the development of extensive and costly intercontinental gas transportation system.
  4. Although other fossil fuels such as oil, sand, heavy oil and oil shale is very large. However they may meet the measure amount of actual requirements.
  5. Even after making vigorous policies the demand of energy will continue to grow. The enhancement of growth must increasingly be satisfied by energy sources other than oil which will be progressively reserved for uses that oil can satisfy.
  6. The electricity from nuclear power is capable of making an important contribution to the global energy supply provided the nuclear fusion techniques are fully developed.
  7. The continued growth of energy demand requires the development of new energy sources. The trend of world economy dominated by oil must stop. It is only possible is alternatives are fully developed. This development may require 10 to 25 years.
  8. Other than hydroelectric power, renewable sources of energy e.g., solar, wind, biomass, tidal, geothermal etc are likely to contribute significant quantities of additional energy during the century at the global level. Although they could be of importance for a particular area. They are likely to become increasingly important during 22 Century.
  9. Implementation of energy conservation techniques may further reduce the energy demand and may narrow the prospective gaps between energy demand and supply. Policies of energy conversation should continue to be key elements of all the future energy planning.
  10. Coal has the potential to contribute substantially to future energy demand. The coal are reserved in abundant but taking advantage of them requires an active programme of development by producers, consumers and Government.

It can be concluded from the above discussion:

  1. Oil production should be level off very shortly.
  2. Alternative sources of energy must be taped and developed fully to meet the demand of increasing population.
  3. Large investment and long lead times are required to produce the energy from alternative sources.
  4. The task for the world would be to manage a transition from dependence on oil to greater reliance on other fossil fuels in short term and on renewable energy system on the long term.

Topic 2.5
Availability of Energy

Energy sources can be broadly categorized as:

  1. Commercial
  2. Non commercial. The fussil fuels comes in the first category and renewable energy sources comes in second category.

Commercial or Conventional or Unrenewable Energy Sources — This can also be subdivided in two categories (i) Major and (ii) Minor sources of energy.

Major Sources of Energy: It includes (a) Fossil fuel (b) Water power and (c) Nuclear energy. Fossil fuels is mostly on solid forms viz., Anthracite, bituminous, brown, light and peats liquid fuel is petroleum and gaseous form is natural gas.

Minor Sources of Energy: It includes sun, wind, tide, biomass, geothermal, ocean, fuel cells, thermoelectric, generators, thermionic etc.
