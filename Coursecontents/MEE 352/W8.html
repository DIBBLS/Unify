Topic 2.17.2
Fuel Cell

A cell or combination of cells are capable to generate an electric current by converting the chemical energy of a fuel directly onto electricity. The chemical energy is the free energy of the reactant used. This conversion takes place at a constant temperature and pressure. As per the fuel used the fuel cell can be classified as:

  1. Hydrogen (H₂) fuel cell
  2. Hydrazine (N₂H₄) fuel cell
  3. Hydrocarbon fuel cell
  4. Alcohol (Methanol) fuel cell

The main components of a hydrogen, oxygen (HYDROX) cell are: (i) A fuel electrode (anode) (ii) An oxidant or air electrode (Cathode) and (iii) An electrolyte

Classification by Temperature:

  1. Low temperature fuel cell — 20-100°C
  2. Medium temperature fuel cell — 100-500°C
  3. High temperature fuel cell — 500-1000°C
  4. Very high temperature fuel cell — more than 1000°C

Classification by Electrolyte:

  1. Gas fuel cell — hydrogen low hydrocarbon
  2. Liquid fuel cell — alcohols, hydrazine, higher hydrocarbon
  3. Solid fuel cell — metals

The fuel cell can be used for: (a) Domestic purpose (b) Central power station (c) Automotive vehicles (d) Special application

Topic 2.17.3
Batteries

It is a combination of individual cell. It can also be called as a black-box containing electrical energy stored electrochemically and later recovered as electrical energy. It is of two types (a) Stored and (b) Charged cell.

The battery can be of two types:

  1. Primary battery (non-chargeable) — chemical reactions are irreversible e.g., dry cell flash light batteries.
  2. Secondary battery (solar, wind electrical energy storage) — the chemical reactions are reversible e. g., lead acid batteries.

Energy efficiency of a battery formula:

ηe = (E₁ × Amp. hour output) / (E₂ × Amp. hour input)

Where: I₁ = Battery discharge current, I₂ = Battery charged current, E₁ = Battery discharge terminal voltage, E₂ = Battery charged terminal voltage, θ₁ = Battery discharge time, θ₂ = Battery charging time.

"Cycle life" is the number of times the battery can be charged/discharged under specified conditions. It vary greatly with depth of discharge/deep discharge tending to short cycle life.

Topic 2.18
Merits and Demerits of Energy Sources

Conventional Energy Sources — Merits:

  1. Cheaper than non-conventional resources. Electricity costs: ₹2.20 from gas, 1.85 from hydro and ₹3.8 from nuclear source on the basic of per kwh.
  2. Storage of raw materials is easy and convenient.
  3. Technology is universally available.

Conventional Energy Sources — Demerits:

  1. Generate pollution — main pollutants are CO, CO₂, NOx, SOx, particulate matter and heat.
  2. Can be used for the production of other chemical petrochemical, paraceutical and paint products. It is necessary to preserve this purposes.
  3. Safety of nuclear plants is controversial — handling nuclear waste is dangerous, accidental leakage of radioactive materials, availability is limited, sophisticated technology is required.
  4. Hydro-electric plants are cleanest but hydro reserver have following problem: ecological disturbances, affect wild life, dislocation of large population, rehabilitation problems.

Non-conventional Energy Sources — Merits: Available in nature free of cost, environment friendly, inexhaustible, low gestation period.

Non-conventional Energy Sources — Demerits: Energy available in dilute form, cost of harnessing energy is generally high, initial cost is high, requires sophisticated equipment.

Topic 2.19
Innovation Energy Harvesting Technology

The scientists have developed an innovative energy harvesting technology which can capture human motion power to charge mobile/electronics devices such as smart phones, laptop, batteries etc.

The energy harvesting and storage technology developed could reduce Reliance on the batteries in the mobile. Technology could enable a footwear embeded energy harvester which captures energy produced by human being while walking and it could be stored for later use.

It could be especially useful for military, as soldiers currently carry heavy batteries to power their Radio, global positioning system (GPS) units, night vision goggles etc in the field.

Human walking carries a lot of energy — theoretical estimates shows that can produce up to 10 watts of electrical power per shoe which is being wasted as heat. A total of 20 watts from walking is not a small thing, especially compared to the power requirements of the majority of the modern mobile device.

The research work at the university of Wisconsin. Madison (U-W madison), U.S.A is in progress to enhance the rate of energy generation.
