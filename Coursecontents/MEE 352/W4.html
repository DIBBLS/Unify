Topic 2.6
India's Industries and Infrastructure

The infrastructure sectors viz., transport, power, telecommunication provide critical input to the manufacturing sectors. The infrastructure services constitute direct input to the production, lowering the costs and improving the quality of these sectors have direct bearing on the improvements in the profitability of the production sectors. The availability and efficient use of infrastructure sectors may help in improving the quality and cost of production sectors. Industrial output growth has closely tracked the movements in the composite index of infrastructure industries during 1980s and 1990s. The observed relationship between infrastructure growth and industrial performance has important implication for sustaining higher output. The average growth of production and composite index of infrastructure in 1980s was 7.8% and 8.4 respectively which declined to 6.5% and 6.3% in 1990s. The main reason of this declined trend was the decline in growth of electricity, coal and petroleum i.e., essentially the energy subsector. Due to this it seems that identification and bridging of gap of infrastructure plays important role in economic growth.

Topic 2.7
Energy Consumption

The energy consumption in 1965 was approximately 53 MTOE (million tons oil equivalent) and per capita consumption was about 110 kg of oil equivalent. By 2010, the primary energy consumption has crossed the 500 m tonne marked roughly 9 times that of 1965. Population during the same period has tripled and thus the per capita energy consumption shows 9 times jump over 1965 level.

Except coal and hydro all other energy sources have increase that gives that the population has become more wise and prefer to use more safe energy sources. The principles of pricing adopted for energy products have greatly influenced the pattern of growth of the energy sector.

Topic 2.8
Exploration Scenario

India's basins have been categorized into four categories based on the prospectivity of the basins:

  1. Established commercial production — seven basins
  2. Known accumulation but no commercial production — two basins
  3. Indicated and geologically prospective — seven basins
  4. Uncertain potential — two basins

Salient Features of NELP (New Exploration Licensing Policy):

  1. No mandatory state participation through ONGC/Oil etc.
  2. All companies have to compete to obtain PEL.
  3. Open availability of exploration average.
  4. The average will be demarcated on a grid system.
  5. Freedom for marketing of crude oil and gas.
  6. Royalty payment for on land and offshore area @ 12.5% and 10% respectively.
  7. Cess has been abolished for the blocks offered.
  8. No import duty on the goods imported.
  9. No signature, discovery and production bonuses.
  10. Seven year tax holiday from the date of commencement.
  11. Fiscal stability to be provided during entire period.
  12. A separate petroleum tax guide to facilitate investors.
  13. A revised model contract.
  14. Royality will be charged at half the prevailing rate for normal off shore areas for deep water.
  15. No royality for the first seven year after commencement for deep water beyond 400 meters.
