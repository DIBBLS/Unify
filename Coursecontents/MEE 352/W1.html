Topic 2.1
General Concept of Energy

The energy is demand of human being for their survival. It is directly related with the population. As the population of the word is increasing everyday and so is demand of energy. It is required to sustain the industrial growth, transportation and human beings comfort. It is well known fact that the known sources of energy i.e., "Fossile Fuel" is depleting very fast and by the turn of the century it may not exist at all. Due to this fact new sources of energy have found an encouraging demand. Energy sources available can be divided into the three type;

  1. Primary energy
  2. Secondary energy
  3. Supplementary energy
Topic 2.1.1
Primary Energy

It provides a net supply of energy. Their energy yield ratio is very high. The yield ratio is "the energy feedback by the materials to the energy received from the environment." The primary sources can only accelerate the growth rate but their supply is limited. It becomes essential to use these fuels sparingly. It contributes considerably to the energy supply.

Active Recall
The energy yield ratio is defined as "the energy feedback by the materials to the energy received from the environment." For primary energy, this ratio is very high, meaning these sources return significantly more energy than is invested in harnessing them. However, their supply is limited, so they must be used sparingly.
Although primary energy sources have a high energy yield ratio and contribute considerably to energy supply, their supply is limited. They cannot be replenished at the rate they are consumed, making conservation essential to ensure long-term availability.
Topic 2.1.2
Secondary Energy

It produces net energy though it may be necessary for the economy, these may not yield net energy. Intensive agricultural is an example where in the terms of energy yield is less than the input.

Active Recall
Primary energy has a very high energy yield ratio — far more energy is returned than is invested. Secondary energy may produce net energy, but in some cases the energy yield is less than the input. Intensive agriculture is given as an example where the energy returned may not exceed what was invested in farming activities.

Topic 2.1.4
Conventional Sources

These are energy sources which are naturally available viz., coal, natural gas, oil and/or nuclear energy. These form the part of primary energy sources. It is also know as unrenewable sources.

Unconventional sources: The secondary sources i.e., solar energy, wind energy, water energy, human energy etc. Categorized as secondary sources. Solar energy can be used through plants, solar cells, solar heater, solar drying and solar tower. These are some of the emerging technologies where solar energy are used. Solar heating/drying is economical application if passive methods are used. Another unconventional sources are wind, tide, wave, hydroelectric, geothermal, ocean thermal etc.

As the unrenewable sources are depleting very fast the coming generation has to depend mostly on renewable sources of energy. It can be classified as:

  1. Solar energy
  2. Wind energy
  3. Energy from biomass
  4. Geothermal energy
  5. Ocean energy (OTEC)
  6. Chemical engg. sources
  7. Magneto hydrodynamic (MHD)
Active Recall
Conventional energy sources are those which are naturally available. They form part of primary energy sources and are also known as unrenewable sources. Examples include: coal, natural gas, oil, and nuclear energy.
The seven renewable energy classifications are: (1) Solar energy, (2) Wind energy, (3) Energy from biomass, (4) Geothermal energy, (5) Ocean energy (OTEC), (6) Chemical engineering sources, (7) Magneto hydrodynamic (MHD).
Conventional sources are naturally available, finite (unrenewable) and form part of primary energy — e.g., coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear energy. Unconventional sources are secondary sources that are renewable — e.g., solar, wind, wave, hydroelectric, geothermal, ocean thermal energy. As conventional sources deplete, future generations must rely on unconventional sources.
