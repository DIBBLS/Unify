Topic 5.1
The Automobile Engine — Overview

Imagine you have a box. You light a small explosion inside the box. The explosion pushes a plunger (piston) down very hard. That plunger is connected to a rod, which turns a spinning shaft. That spinning motion eventually turns your car's wheels. That's essentially what an engine does — it converts burning fuel into rotation.

The engine is the heart of your car. It is a complex machine built to convert heat from burning gas into the force that turns the road wheels.

Topic 5.2
Engine Nomenclature — Key Terms

Before you can understand engine performance, you need to know its vocabulary. These terms are like coordinates on a map — without them, you can't describe where things are or what's happening inside the engine.

1. Bore: The inside diameter of the cylinder is called bore.

2. Stroke: The linear distance along the cylinder axis between two limiting positions is called the stroke.

3. Top Dead Centre (T.D.C.): The topmost position of the piston towards the cover end side of the cylinder is called T.D.C.

4. Bottom Dead Centre (B.D.C.): The lowest position of the piston towards the crank end side of the cylinder is called B.D.C.

5. Clearance Volume: The volume contained in the cylinder above the top of the piston, when the piston is at top dead centre, is called the clearance volume.

6. Swept Volume: The volume swept by the piston while moving between T.D.C. to B.D.C. is called swept volume or piston displacement.

7. Compression Ratio: It is the ratio of Total cylinder volume to clearance volume.
Compression Ratio Formula
CR = Total Cylinder Volume / Clearance Volume
CR = (Swept Volume + Clearance Volume) / Clearance Volume
  • CR = Compression Ratio (dimensionless)
  • Swept Volume = Volume displaced by piston from BDC to TDC
  • Clearance Volume = Volume above piston when at TDC
Worked Example

Problem: An engine has a total cylinder volume of 600 cc and a clearance volume of 50 cc. Calculate the compression ratio.

Total cylinder volume = 600 cc; Clearance volume = 50 cc
Swept Volume = Total – Clearance = 600 – 50 = 550 cc
CR = Total / Clearance = 600 / 50 = 12:1

A CR of 12:1 is typical for a diesel engine (diesel engines have higher CR than petrol engines, typically 14:1 to 25:1, vs. petrol at 8:1 to 12:1).

Common Mistake
Students calculate CR as Swept Volume / Clearance Volume. This gives the WRONG answer. The correct numerator is TOTAL cylinder volume (swept + clearance), not just swept volume. CR = (Swept Volume + Clearance Volume) / Clearance Volume.

Topic 5.3
Cylinder Block and Cylinder Head

The engine is divided into two main housing sections. The cylinder block is the lower, heavy part that contains the pistons and crankshaft. The cylinder head sits on top and contains the valves and combustion chambers. Think of a pot (block) with a lid (head) — combustion happens in the space between them.

Cylinder Block: A cast structure with cylindrical holes bored to guide and support the pistons and to harness the working gases. It also provides a jacket to contain a liquid coolant. The block houses the crankshaft, which converts the reciprocating motion of the pistons into rotary motion at the crankshaft flywheel interface. Often also houses the camshaft.

Cylinder Head: This casting encloses the combustion end of the cylinder block and houses both the inlet and exhaust poppet-valves and their ports to admit air–fuel mixture and to exhaust the combustion products. Contains valve-controlled passages through which the air and fuel mixture enters the cylinders, and others through which the gases produced by their combustion are expelled.
