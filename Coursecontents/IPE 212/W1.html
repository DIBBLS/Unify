Topic 1.1
Definition of an Automobile

Imagine a box that can move on its own without being pushed or pulled. That's basically what an automobile is — a machine with its own engine that moves itself. "Auto" means self and "mobile" means movable, so automobile literally means "self-movable." It's different from a boat (marine vehicle) or a plane (aeronautical vehicle) because it moves on the ground.

Exam-ready definition — Automobile
A self-propelled vehicle which contains the power source for its propulsion and is used for carrying passengers and goods on the ground, such as car, bus, trucks, etc. The word "automotive" means a vehicle which can move by itself.
Real-World Example
A Toyota Camry, a Mack truck, and a delivery van are all automobiles — each has its own engine providing the power to move itself. They differ from aircraft (which fly) and boats (which sail on water).
Common Mistake
Students often confuse "automobile" with any vehicle. Remember: an automobile specifically uses a self-contained power source and moves on the ground. A bicycle is not an automobile (no engine). A ship is not an automobile (water-borne).

Topic 1.2
Components of an Automobile

Think of an automobile like a team where every player has a specific job. The frame is the skeleton, the engine is the heart, the transmission passes the power to the wheels, and the body is the outer skin everyone sees.

Five main components
The main components of an automobile are: Frame (supporting the body), Engine (supplies the power), Power transmitting units, Tyres and wheels (through the springs and axles), and Body.

Topic 1.3
Basis of Motion & Vehicle Construction

How does a car actually move? The engine creates power, but it's the friction between the tyres and the road that actually pushes the car forward. If there were no friction (like on perfectly smooth ice), the wheels would just spin in place. The vehicle's structure goes from the inside out: Frame + Base components = Chassis. Chassis + Body = Vehicle.

Basis of motion
The automobile is propelled through the frictional contact between the road and the wheels. The various units are held together in proper arrangement on the frame. The protection and comfort is provided by the body of the vehicle.
Construction Equation
"Frame" + "Base components" = "Chassis"
"Chassis" + "Body" = "Vehicle"
Frame: The skeleton of the vehicle — the main foundation and base for alignment. Types: Conventional frame, Semi-integral frame, Integral frame.
Chassis: Frame + base components (engine, radiator, clutch, gearbox, silencer, road wheels, fuel tank, wiring, differential units, etc.)
Body: The superstructure of the vehicle bolted to the chassis. Types: Car, Truck, Tractor, Delivery van, Jeep, Bus.
Common Mistake
Students say "the chassis IS the frame." Wrong — the frame is just the skeleton. The chassis includes the frame PLUS all base mechanical components (engine, gearbox, wheels, etc.). Think of the frame as the bones and the chassis as the bones plus all the organs.
