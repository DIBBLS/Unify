Week 8 of 12 Piston Engine Operations & Engine Classification

The four-stroke cycle is the fundamental operating sequence of most automobile engines. This week explains each stroke in detail, followed by a comprehensive classification of engines by fuel type, cylinder number, arrangement, operating cycle, and ignition type.

Topic 8.1
The Four-Stroke Engine Cycle

A four-stroke engine is like a pump that goes through 4 steps to make power. Breathe in (intake) → squeeze (compression) → explode (power) → blow out (exhaust). The piston goes up and down twice (two complete up-down cycles = four strokes) to complete one full power cycle.

A four-stroke engine must undergo four different strokes to complete one cycle to produce power. The four strokes are: Intake, Compression, Power, Exhaust.

Intake Stroke: The cam motion triggers the opening of the inlet valve creating a suction; hence the air-fuel mixture is entered the cylinder giving room for the second stroke as the inlet valve closes.

Compression Stroke: Both the inlet valve and the compression valve remain closed. The air-fuel is further compressed to build up more pressure within the cylinder. At the end of this stroke, compressed air-fuel is ignited by the spark plug.

Power Stroke: The explosion exerts pressure and pushes the piston down, giving power to the crank.

Exhaust Stroke: The outlet valve opens and pushes out the burnt gas. This marks the end of a cycle and the whole process is repeated over and over again keeping the engine running and producing power.

Topic 8.2
Classification of Automobile Engines
1. Based on Fuel Type:
— Internal Combustion Engines (ICE): Petrol Engines (use gasoline and spark ignition); Diesel Engines (use diesel fuel and compression ignition); Alternative Fuel Engines (CNG, LPG, other fuels).
— Electric Motors: Electric vehicles (EVs) use batteries to power electric motors.
— Hybrid Engines: Combine an ICE with an electric motor. Mild Hybrid (MHEV), Hybrid Electric Vehicle (HEV), Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV).

2. Based on Number of Cylinders:
Single-cylinder (motorcycles); Twin-cylinder; Three-cylinder (fuel-efficient cars); Four-cylinder (most common); Six-cylinder; Eight-cylinder (V8); Ten-cylinder (V10); Twelve-cylinder (V12) (high-performance and luxury).

3. Based on Cylinder Arrangement:
— Straight/Inline: Cylinders in a single line.
— V-engine: Cylinders in two banks forming a "V" shape.
— Flat or Boxer Engine: Cylinders arranged horizontally opposite each other.
— W-engine: Combines two V-engines, creating a "W" shape.

4. Based on Operating Cycle:
— Two-stroke engine: Completes the combustion cycle in two strokes of the piston.
— Four-stroke engine: Completes the combustion cycle in four strokes of the piston.

5. Based on Ignition Type:
— Spark Ignition (SI) engine: Uses a spark plug to ignite the air-fuel mixture (gasoline engines).
— Compression Ignition (CI) engine: Compresses the air-fuel mixture to a high temperature, causing it to ignite (diesel engines).

6. Other Classifications:
— Naturally Aspirated: Relies on atmospheric pressure to fill the cylinders.
— Turbocharged: Uses an exhaust-driven turbine to force more air into the cylinders.
— Supercharged: Uses a mechanical compressor to force more air in.
— Air-cooled vs. Water-cooled: Refers to the method of cooling the engine.
Common Mistake
Students confuse SI and CI engines by saying "diesel engines use spark plugs." Wrong — diesel (CI) engines do NOT have spark plugs. They ignite fuel by compressing air to such a high temperature that injected diesel spontaneously combusts. Spark plugs are only in petrol (SI) engines.
