Week 1 of 12 Velocity of Points on Rigid Links

This week introduces kinematics — the study of motion without caring about forces. You'll learn how to find the velocity of any point on a moving link using equations and graphical methods. This is the starting point for everything in mechanisms.

Topic 1.1
What is Kinematics? Velocity basics

Kinematics is the study of motion. It doesn't ask "why is it moving?" — it only asks "how is it moving?". Think of it like watching a car from above: you can see it speeding up, slowing down, turning — but you don't know or care about the engine inside.

Velocity is a characteristic of kinematics. When a rigid link rotates, every single point on it moves — but they all move at different speeds depending on how far they are from the centre of rotation.

Equations of motion — constant acceleration
v = u + at
v² = u² + 2as
s = ut + ½at²
v = final velocity (m/s) · u = initial velocity (m/s) · a = acceleration (m/s²) · t = time (s) · s = displacement (m)
Velocity of a point on a rotating link
V_A = (O₁A) × ω₂
V_A = velocity of point A (m/s) · O₁A = distance from centre of rotation to point A (m) · ω₂ = angular velocity of the link (rad/s)
Rotating link — velocity of a point
Real-world example
Think of a ceiling fan. The centre (O₁) is fixed to the ceiling. Each blade tip is like point A. The longer the blade, the faster that tip moves — even though the whole fan rotates at the same ω. That's exactly what V_A = ω × r tells you.
Common mistake
Students forget that V acts perpendicular to the link, not along it. If you draw your velocity arrow pointing along the rod, that's wrong. It must be at 90° to the link direction.
