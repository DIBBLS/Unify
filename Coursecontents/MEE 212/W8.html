Topic 8.1
Gear Trains — Definition & Types
Exam-ready definition (from notes)
A gear train is a mechanical system consisting of 2 or more gears used to transmit motion, speed & direction. It's all about putting gears together and each one has its own shaft.
Four types of gear trains (from notes)
1. Simple gear train — shafts are parallel and do not intersect
2. Compound gear train — one shaft carries (shares) 2 gears
3. Reverted gear train — input and output shafts are on the same line
4. Epicyclic gear train — gears rotate around other gears (also called Planetary gear system)
Epicyclic consists of: Ring/Angular gear · Planet gears · Sun gear
Idler gear (from notes)
An idler gear is placed between 2 other gears (usually driver and driven) and does NOT change the gear ratio.
It moves in opposite direction. It changes the direction of the driven gear and bridges distance between gears that are far apart.
Types of gear trains — schematic
Speed ratio formula
Speed Ratio = N₁/N₂ = Z₂/Z₁
N₁ = speed of driver gear (rpm) · N₂ = speed of driven gear (rpm) · Z₁ = number of teeth on driver · Z₂ = number of teeth on driven · High gear ratio → high torque, low speed, bigger teeth · Low gear ratio → low torque, high speed, smaller teeth
Real-world example
A bicycle has gears. When you shift to a small front gear and big rear gear (low gear ratio), the wheel turns slower but you can climb hills easily (high torque). When you shift to high gear, the wheel spins fast (high speed) but you need to pedal harder on hills. That's exactly what gear ratio does.
Common mistake
Students write N₁/N₂ = Z₁/Z₂ (wrong — teeth and speed are inversely proportional). More teeth = slower speed. The correct formula is N₁/N₂ = Z₂/Z₁.
