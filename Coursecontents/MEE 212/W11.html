Week 11 of 12 Power Transmission & Belt Drives — Theory

Belt drives are one of the most common ways to transmit power — they're on bicycles, washing machines, car engines. This week covers the theory: open vs cross belts, slip, creep, angle of contact, velocity ratio, and the all-important tension ratio.

  3. Calculate velocity ratio including slip. Know the difference between open and cross belt angle of contact formulas.
Topic 11.1
Power Transmission & Belt Drive Theory
Exam-ready definition (from notes)
Power transmission is used to transfer power from the prime mover to the moving machine [source to driven machine]. A transmission system is the combination of clutch and gearbox. It is a mechanical device which varies the engine torque according to the driver requirement.
Types of belt drives (from notes)
1. Flat belt · 2. V-belt · 3. Tooth belt
Open belt system: pulleys rotate in the SAME direction
Cross belt system: pulleys rotate in OPPOSITE directions (belt crosses itself)
Key definitions (from notes)
Creep: extension of belt on tight side and contraction at slack side
Slip: relative motion between pulley and belt, expressed as percentage (ε = 0.01 to 0.03)
Optimum angle of contact: 90°–170°
Velocity ratio with slip
N_s/N_L = D_L/D_s × [1 − S₁/100] × [1 − S₂/100]
Without slip: N₁/N₂ = D₂/D₁
N_s = speed of smaller pulley (rpm) · N_L = speed of larger pulley · D_L = diameter of large pulley · D_s = diameter of small pulley · S₁, S₂ = slip percentages
Angle of contact (wrap angle)
Open belt: β = sin⁻¹[(D_L − D_s) / 2C]
θ_s = 180° − 2β (angle on small pulley)
θ_L = 180° + 2β (angle on large pulley)
Cross belt: θ_s = θ_L = 180° + 2β
C = centre-to-centre distance · β = angle of lap · Use smaller θ in tension ratio formula
Tension ratio (key formula)
T₁/T₂ = e^(μθ)
(T₁ − Tc) / (T₂ − Tc) = e^(μθ) [including centrifugal tension]
Tc = mv² [centrifugal tension]
T₁ = tension in tight side · T₂ = tension in slack side · Tc = centrifugal tension · μ = coefficient of friction · θ = angle of contact (radians)
Power transmitted & belt length
Power = (T₁ − T₂) × V
V = πDN/60 (belt velocity, m/s)
L = 2C + 1.57(D_L + D_s) + (D_L − D_s)²/4C [Open belt]
C = centre distance (m) · D_L, D_s = pulley diameters (m)
Open belt vs cross belt — angle of contact
Common mistake
Using the LARGE pulley's angle of contact in the tension ratio. Always use the SMALLER angle (which is on the smaller pulley for open belts). The belt slips first where the wrap angle is smallest. Using the wrong θ gives a completely wrong tension ratio.
