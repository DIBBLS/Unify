Week 3 of 12 Acceleration in Mechanisms

Velocity was one thing. Now we go one step further — acceleration. Every point on a moving link has two components of acceleration: one pointing toward the centre (normal/centripetal) and one along the rotation direction (tangential). You'll learn to find both and use them in 4-bar problems.

Exam focus
  1. Given a 4-bar mechanism with ω and α, find the acceleration of points on all links. Common exam Q
  2. Distinguish between normal and tangential acceleration and write their formulas.
  3. Find the angular acceleration of a link from an acceleration diagram.
Topic 3.1
Normal and Tangential Acceleration

When a link rotates, a point on it accelerates in two ways at the same time. Imagine you're on a roundabout: you feel pushed outward (that's the normal/centripetal part) and if the roundabout is speeding up, you also feel pushed backward (that's the tangential part).

The two acceleration components
aⁿ = rω² (normal — points toward centre, always exists if ω ≠ 0)
aᵗ = rα (tangential — perpendicular to link, only if α ≠ 0)
r = length of link (m) · ω = angular velocity (rad/s) · α = angular acceleration (rad/s²)
Acceleration of B relative to A (vector equation)
a_B = a_A + a_B/A
a_Bⁿ + a_Bᵗ = a_Aⁿ + a_Aᵗ + a_B/Aⁿ + a_B/Aᵗ
aⁿ = normal (centripetal) component pointing inward · aᵗ = tangential component perpendicular to link
Normal vs tangential acceleration components on a link
Worked Example — 4-bar mechanism (from notes 28-05-2025)

AB = 170mm, BC = OD = 280mm, AD = 500mm, ∠BAD = 55°. Crank AB rotates at 100 rad/s clockwise. Find acceleration of links BC & OD, and angular acceleration of BC & CD.

Given: ω_AB = 100 rad/s, α_AB = 0 (constant speed)
Step 1 — Normal accel of A: a_Aⁿ = r × ω² = 0.17 × 100² = 1700 m/s² (toward O)
Step 2 — a_B/A normal: a_B/Aⁿ = AB × ω_AB² = 0.17 × 10000 = 1700 m/s²
Step 3 — a_B/A tangential: a_B/Aᵗ = AB × α = unknown (draw perpendicular to AB)
Step 4: Draw acceleration diagram. Measure unknowns from diagram. Find α_BC = b'b / BC
Worked Example — Classwork 26-05-2024

AB=75mm, BC=80mm, AD=125mm, BE=28mm. ω=100 rad/s, α=4400 rad/s²

V_A = (AB)ω = 0.075 × 100 = 7.5 m/s
aⁿ_AB = r × ω² = 0.075 × 100² = 750 m/s²
aᵗ_AB = r × α = 0.075 × 4400 = 330 m/s²
aⁿ_BC = V²_BC / BC = (10.75)² / 0.075 = 1540 m/s² (approx)
Common mistake
Forgetting that aⁿ_B/A acts from B toward A (inward), while aᵗ_B/A acts perpendicular to link BA. Drawing them in the wrong direction ruins your entire acceleration diagram.
