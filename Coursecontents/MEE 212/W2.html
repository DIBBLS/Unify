Week 2 of 12 Relative Velocity Method

Here you learn how to find the velocity of a point by relating it to another known point. This "relative velocity" idea is the engine behind all graphical mechanism analysis. Master this and acceleration problems become much easier too.

Topic 2.1
The Relative Velocity Principle

Here's the key idea: if you know the velocity of point A, you can find the velocity of point B by adding the velocity of B relative to A. It's like saying: "I know how fast the bus is going. To find how fast someone running on the bus is going, I add their running speed to the bus speed."

Relative velocity equations
V_BA = V_B − V_A
∴ V_B = V_A + V_BA
V_B = (O₂B) × ω_f
ω_f = V_B / (O₂B)
V_BA = velocity of B relative to A · V_A = absolute velocity of A · V_B = absolute velocity of B · ω_f = angular velocity of the link containing B · O₂B = length of that link
Angular velocity of a link (from velocity diagram)
ω_a = V_BA / (AB)
V_BA = length of the relative velocity vector in the diagram · AB = length of the actual link
4-bar mechanism velocity diagram
Worked Example — From your notes (16-05-2025)

A 4-bar mechanism: O₁A = 380mm, O₂B = 250mm, AC = 250mm, BC = 400mm. Link 2 rotates at 150 rev/min. Find velocity of C & A, angular speed of links 3 & 4.

Step 1: Convert speed → ω₂ = 2π(150)/60 = 15.71 rad/s
Step 2: V_A = (O₁A) × ω₂ = 0.38 × 15.71 = 5.97 m/s (perpendicular to O₁A)
Step 3: Draw velocity diagram to scale. V_B = V_A + V_BA
Step 4: ω_f = V_B / (O₂B) — read V_B from diagram, divide by link length.
Common mistake
Not converting rev/min to rad/s before calculating. Always do: ω = 2πN/60. If N = 150 rpm → ω = 15.71 rad/s, NOT 150.
