Week 4 of 11 Armature Windings, Pitches & The EMF Equation

The armature winding generates EMF in a generator and experiences force in a motor. Understanding lap vs wave winding and the EMF equation is core exam material. You must be able to derive the EMF equation from first principles.

Topic 4.1
Lap vs Wave & The EMF Equation

Lap winding: parallel paths a = P (number of poles). More paths → more current capacity. High current, lower voltage. Used in large low-voltage machines.

Wave winding: parallel paths a = 2 always, regardless of poles. Two long series strings → higher voltage. High voltage, lower current. Used in high-voltage machines. No equalising connections needed.

Pitches: For simplex progressive lap winding: Yb − Yf = 2. Average pitch: (Yb+Yf)/2 = C/P, so Yb+Yf = 2C/P (C = total coil sides, P = poles).

EMF Equation — DC Generator
Eɡ = (φ × Z × N × P) / (60 × a)
φ = flux per pole (Wb) · Z = total armature conductors · N = speed (rpm)
P = number of poles · a = parallel paths
Lap: a = P    Wave: a = 2 (always)
Worked Example 1 — 6-pole lap
P=6, a=6 (lap), Z=720, φ=0.04Wb, N=400rpm

Eɡ = (0.04×720×400×6)/(60×6) = 69120/360

Eɡ = 192 V
Worked Example 2 — 8-pole wave, find speed
P=8, a=2 (wave), Z=1000, φ=0.035Wb, Eɡ=500V. N = 60aEɡ/(φZP)

N = (60×2×500)/(0.035×1000×8) = 60000/280

N = 214.3 rpm
Worked Example 3 — 4-pole, 24-slot lap pitches
C=24 coil sides, P=4. Yb+Yf = 2C/P = 48/4 = 12. Progressive: Yb−Yf = 2.

2Yb=14 → Yb=7 · 2Yf=10 → Yf=5

Back pitch Yb = 7  ·  Front pitch Yf = 5
Most common mistake in this topic
Wrong value of a. For lap: a=P. For wave: a=2 ALWAYS — not 2P, not P/2, just 2. In an 8-pole machine this is a factor of 4 error. Also: Yb+Yf = 2C/P not C/P — the factor of 2 is easy to miss.
