Week 10 of 11 Induction Motors — Slip, Speed & Power Flow

Induction motors are the workhorses of industry — over 90% of all industrial motors. Rugged, cheap, AC supply direct. The key insight: the rotor never catches the rotating magnetic field. That gap (slip) is what produces torque.

Topic 10.1
Slip, Speed, Power Flow & Motor Types

3-phase AC in stator → rotating magnetic field at Ns. Field induces EMF in rotor conductors → rotor current → Lorentz force → torque. Rotor chases the field but can never catch it: if N=Ns, no relative motion → no EMF → no current → no torque → rotor slows down. So s is always >0 in normal operation.

Rotor frequency fr = s×f. At standstill (s=1): fr = supply frequency. At normal speed (s=0.04): fr = 2Hz.

Power flow: Air gap power P_ag reaches rotor. Rotor copper loss = s×P_ag. Mechanical power = (1−s)×P_ag. Rotor efficiency = N/Ns = 1−s. Low slip → small rotor copper loss → high efficiency.

SCIM (Squirrel Cage): copper bars short-circuited at ends. Rugged, no maintenance, cheap. Fixed resistance. WRIM (Wound Rotor): full 3-phase winding, external resistance via slip rings. Variable speed and high starting torque at cost of complexity.

Induction motor equations
Ns = 120f / P     s = (Ns−N)/Ns     N = Ns(1−s)
fr = s × f     Rotor copper loss = s × P_ag
P_mech = (1−s) × P_ag     Rotor η = 1−s = N/Ns
Worked Example

4-pole, 3-phase, 50Hz. Find: (i) Ns (ii) N at s=4% (iii) fr at N=1200rpm.

Ns = 120×50/4 = 1500 rpm

N = 1500×(1−0.04) = 1440 rpm

s at 1200rpm = (1500−1200)/1500 = 0.2 · fr = 0.2×50 = 10Hz

Ns=1500rpm · N=1440rpm · fr=10Hz at 1200rpm
Common mistake
P in Ns=120f/P is total poles, NOT pole pairs. 4-pole motor: P=4, Ns=1500rpm. Using P=2 gives 3000rpm — double the correct answer. Always use total number of poles.
