Week 9 of 11 AC Circuits — RMS, Impedance & Power Factor

AC circuits are the backbone of power systems. R, L, C behave very differently in AC. Impedance and power factor are the two concepts that unlock everything. The RMS value is what actually matters for power calculations.

Topic 9.1
AC Parameters, Impedance & Power Factor

AC voltage: e(t) = Eₘsin(ωt+φ). RMS = Eₘ/√2 = 0.707Eₘ — DC equivalent for same average power. "230V mains" = 230V RMS; actual peak = 325V. Average (half-cycle) = 0.637Eₘ.

R: opposes at all frequencies, V and I in phase. XL = 2πfL — increases with f, voltage leads current 90°. XC = 1/(2πfC) — decreases with f, current leads voltage 90°. Total impedance Z = √(R²+X²), V = IZ.

Power factor cosφ = R/Z = P/S. Lagging = inductive load. Leading = capacitive load. Low PF → more current needed for same real power → more I²R line losses. Utilities penalise industrial customers for low PF.

AC circuit equations
E_rms = Eₘ/√2    XL = 2πfL    XC = 1/(2πfC)
Z = √(R² + X²)    V = IZ    cosφ = R/Z
P = VIcosφ (W)    Q = VIsinφ (VAR)    S = VI (VA)
S² = P² + Q²
Worked Example — RL Series Circuit

R=30Ω, L=0.1H, V=230V, f=50Hz. Find Z, I, PF, VR, VL.

XL = 2π×50×0.1 = 31.42Ω

Z = √(30²+31.42²) = √1887.2 = 43.44Ω

I = 230/43.44 = 5.30A · cosφ = 30/43.44 = 0.691 lagging

VR = 5.30×30 = 159V · VL = 5.30×31.42 = 166.5V

Check: √(159²+166.5²) = 230V ✓

Z=43.44Ω · I=5.30A · PF=0.691 lagging · VR=159V · VL=166.5V
The most common AC mistake
Adding VR + VL directly: 159+166.5=325.5V ≠ 230V. You CANNOT add out-of-phase AC voltages arithmetically. They are 90° apart — use Pythagoras: V = √(VR²+VL²). This costs marks in almost every exam cohort.
