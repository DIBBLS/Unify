Week 6 of 11 DC Motors — Back EMF, Torque & Speed Control

DC motors convert electrical energy into mechanical rotation. Back EMF is the self-regulating mechanism. Torque and speed are deeply connected through flux and armature current. These are the most examined topics in ECE 210.

Topic 6.1
Back EMF, Torque, Speed & Maximum Power

A rotating armature generates back EMF Eb = kNφ opposing supply voltage V. Motor equation: V = Eb + IaRa. At start-up Eb=0 → huge Ia=V/Ra — need starting resistors. As speed builds, Eb rises, Ia falls to normal operating level.

Torque: T = 0.159×Z×φ×P×(Ia/a). So T ∝ φIa. Shunt: φ constant → T ∝ Ia. Series: φ ∝ Ia → T ∝ Ia² (huge starting torque).

Speed: N ∝ Eb/φ = (V−IaRa)/φ. Reduce φ → speed increases (used in speed control).

Maximum power condition: P = Eb×Ia = (V−IaRa)×Ia. Differentiate: dP/dIa = V−2IaRa = 0 → Eb = V/2. Maximum power when back EMF = half supply voltage. (Efficiency only 50% at this point — not the efficient operating point.)

DC Motor equations
V = Eb + IaRa    Ia = (V−Eb)/Ra
T = 0.159×Z×φ×P×(Ia/a)    T ∝ φIa
N ∝ Eb/φ    N₂ = N₁×(Eb₂/Eb₁)×(φ₁/φ₂)
Max power: Eb = V/2    Pmax = V²/(4Ra)
Worked Example — Torque + Speed (from lecture notes)

15kW, 230V, 1150rpm, 4-pole, Z=882, a=4, Ra=0.2Ω, Ia=73A. Find torque. Then new speed if φ₂=0.8φ₁ at same torque.

Eb₁ = V−IaRa = 230−(73×0.2) = 215.4V

φ₁ = (Eb×60×a)/(Z×N×P) = (215.4×60×4)/(882×1150×4) = 0.01274 Wb

T = 0.159×882×0.01274×4×(73/4) = 0.159×882×0.01274×73

T = 130.4 Nm
Same torque: φ₁Ia₁ = φ₂Ia₂ → Ia₂ = 73/0.8 = 91.25A

Eb₂ = 230−(91.25×0.2) = 211.75V

N₂ = 1150×(211.75/215.4)×(1/0.8) = 1150×0.983×1.25

N₂ ≈ 1413 rpm  ·  Reducing flux increases speed
Common mistake
Generator: V = E−IaRa. Motor: V = Eb+IaRa. These are opposite signs — do not swap them. Also: when comparing two operating points, Eb changes at each point (because Ia changed). Always recalculate Eb separately at each operating condition.
Exam-ready — series motor no-load danger
A series motor must never run on no-load because at no load Ia → 0 → φ → 0. Since N ∝ Eb/φ and φ approaches zero, speed increases without limit — the motor runs away and can reach destructive speeds causing mechanical failure. Series motors must always be permanently coupled to their load.
