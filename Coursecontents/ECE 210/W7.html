Week 7 of 11 Transformers — Working Principle & Turns Ratio

A transformer is the most elegant device in electrical engineering — no moving parts, over 98% efficiency. The turns ratio unlocks everything. It works ONLY on AC: DC gives constant flux → dφ/dt=0 → zero secondary EMF.

Topic 7.1
Working Principle, Turns Ratio & Referred Impedance

AC in primary → changing flux in core → changing flux links secondary → EMF induced. No direct electrical connection. Only works on AC because steady DC gives constant flux (dφ/dt=0) → no induced EMF → primary burns.

Turns ratio k = N₁/N₂ = E₁/E₂ = V₁/V₂. Current transforms inversely: I₁/I₂ = N₂/N₁ = 1/k. Power conserved: V₁I₁ = V₂I₂ (ideal).

Referred impedance: Z₂' = k²×Z₂ (secondary → primary). Z₁' = Z₁/k² (primary → secondary). Moving to HV side: multiply by k². Moving to LV side: divide by k².

Transformer equations
k = N₁/N₂ = V₁/V₂     I₁/I₂ = 1/k
Z₂' = k² × Z₂    (refer to primary)
Z₁' = Z₁ / k²    (refer to secondary)
k > 1 → step-down · k < 1 → step-up
Core type: flux passes through one limb, windings on same limb
Shell type: flux divides, windings surrounded by core — better for LV high current
Worked Example 1 — Referred Impedance
k=100, R_load=100Ω on secondary. R' = k²×R = 10000×100

R' = 1 MΩ (100Ω looks like 1MΩ from primary — audio amplifier matching principle)
Worked Example 2 — 100kVA, 1100/220V
k = 1100/220 = 5. R₂' = k²×R₂ = 25×0.004 = 0.1Ω

Req(HV) = R₁+R₂' = 0.1+0.1 = 0.2Ω

R₁' = R₁/k² = 0.1/25 = 0.004Ω

Req(LV) = R₁'+R₂ = 0.004+0.004 = 0.008Ω

Req(HV)=0.2Ω · Req(LV)=0.008Ω · Check: 0.2/0.008=25=k² ✓
Common mistake
Moving LV→HV: multiply by k². Moving HV→LV: divide by k². Students multiply in both directions. Also never say "transformer works on DC if powerful enough" — DC transformers do not exist.
