Week 5 of 11 DC Generators — Types, Equations & Parallel Operation

DC generators convert mechanical rotation into DC electricity. The key differences between types are how they supply their own field current — this determines their voltage-load characteristics. Every type uses the same core equation V = E − IaRa.

Topic 5.1
Generator Types, Equations & Parallel Operation

Core equation for all generators: V = E − IaRa. Terminal voltage V is always less than generated EMF E because Ra drops some voltage.

Generator types — field connection and current equations
Separately-Excited: External field supply. If independent of armature. V = E − IaRa. Best control.
Shunt: Field in parallel. If = V/Rf. Ia = IL + If. Voltage falls with load (cascading: IL↑ → V↓ → If↓ → φ↓ → E↓ → V↓ further).
Series: Field in series with load. If = Ia = IL. V = E − Ia(Ra+Rf). Voltage rises with load. Not for constant voltage supply.
Compound: Both series + shunt fields. Cumulative compound → flat voltage curve. Most practical type.
Worked Example — Shunt Generator Ra

Rf=200Ω, P_out=120kW, V=500V, E=525V. Find Ra.

IL = P/V = 120000/500 = 240A

If = V/Rf = 500/200 = 2.5A

Ia = IL+If = 242.5A · Ra = (E−V)/Ia = 25/242.5

Ra = 0.103 Ω
Exam-ready — four conditions for parallel operation
(1) Terminal voltages must be equal. (2) Polarities must be the same (+to+, −to−). (3) Prime movers must have similar stable speed-load characteristics. (4) The rate of change of voltage with load must be of the same order for all generators.
Common mistake
In a shunt generator, Ia = IL + If (armature supplies both load AND field). Forgetting If makes Ra wrong. Always draw the circuit and identify all current branches before writing any equation.
