Topic 3.1
The Eight Main Parts & What Each Does

Every DC machine has a stator (stationary) and a rotor/armature (rotating), separated by an air gap through which flux crosses.

Parts and functions
Frame/Yoke — outer steel shell. Mechanical support + magnetic return path. Cast iron (small) or cast steel (large).
Field Poles + Coils — bolted inside yoke. Wide pole shoe concentrates flux evenly. Field coils carry current to create MMF. Made of laminated steel.
Interpoles (Commutating Poles) — small poles between main poles. Carry armature current. Improve commutation and reduce brush sparking.
Armature Core — rotating iron cylinder. Thin laminations (0.3–0.5mm) of silicon steel to reduce eddy current loss.
Armature Winding — coils in armature slots. EMF induced here (generator) or force produced here (motor).
Commutator — copper segments separated by mica. Converts alternating EMF in rotating coils into unidirectional DC at terminals.
Brush Gear — stationary carbon brushes on commutator. Collect current (generator) or supply it (motor). Carbon: self-lubricating, low contact resistance.
Commutator analogy
The commutator is a smart switch that flips connections every half revolution — always maintaining the same polarity at the terminals. It straightens the AC generated in the rotating coils into DC output.
Exam-ready — commutator function (full marks answer)
The commutator converts the alternating EMF induced in the rotating armature conductors into a unidirectional (DC) voltage at the external terminals by mechanically reversing the connections to the armature coils at the instant they pass through the magnetic neutral axis.
The mistake that loses a mark every exam
"Laminations reduce hysteresis loss." WRONG. Laminations reduce eddy current loss. Hysteresis loss is reduced by using silicon steel. Different problem, different solution. Examiners test this every year.
