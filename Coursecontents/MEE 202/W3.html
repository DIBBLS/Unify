Week 3 Quantum Numbers & Electron Configuration

The Schrödinger equation gives us three integers — n, l, m — that fully describe an electron's state. Add spin and you have four quantum numbers. These numbers govern the periodic table and determine how elements behave.

Topic 3.1 The Four Quantum Numbers

The solutions to the three-dimensional Schrödinger equation are three sets of integers: n, l, and m. Each combination of n, l, and m is a unique quantum state called an orbital. Each orbital can have a maximum of two electrons, and the two electrons must have opposite spin.

Quantum NumberNameWhat it determinesPermissible Values
n Principal Total energy of the electron; which shell it's in. As n increases, energy decreases (less negative) and approaches zero at infinity. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 … n
l Azimuthal (Angular momentum) Shape and energy of the sub-level. Describes how the angular momentum of the electron is quantized. s, p, d, f correspond to l = 0, 1, 2, 3 0, 1, 2, 3 … (n−1)
m Magnetic The angle between the electron's angular momentum vector and an applied magnetic field. Has only a small effect on energy. −l … 0 … +l
Mₛ Spin The spin of each electron — either "up" or "down." The quantum number of the spin of each electron is usually denoted Mₛ. +½ or −½ only
Quantum Numbers for n = 1, 2, 3
n=1: l=0 (s), m=0 → 1 orbital, max 2 electrons [1s²]
n=2: l=0 (s): m=0; l=1 (p): m=−1,0,+1 → 4 orbitals, max 8 electrons [2s²2p⁶]
n=3: l=0,1,2 (s,p,d): m values from −l to +l → 9 orbitals, max 18 electrons

Filling order (Aufbau): 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p → 5s → 4d…
Note: 4s fills before 3d because the energy of the 4s orbital is lower than the energy of the 3d orbital.
💡 The s, p, d, f Letters
The letters s, p, d, f come from spectroscopy — they represent the lines which give rise to sharp, principal, diffuse and fundamental lines respectively. l=0 → s (spherically symmetrical, like a ball); l=1 → p (dumbbell, 3 orientations); l=2 → d (more complex, 5 orientations); l=3 → f (most complex, 7 orientations).
⚠ Common Mistake
Students confuse the total number of permissible m values with the total number of electrons. The total number of permissible m values gives the total number of orbitals while the total number of Mₛ values gives the total number of electrons in the atom. Each orbital holds max 2 electrons.
★ Why This Matters
The 2021/22 paper asks you to complete a table of quantum numbers for n=1 and n=2. For n=2, you need: l can be 0 or 1. When l=0: m=0 only. When l=1: m=−1, 0, +1. Each combination is one orbital. Total orbitals for n=2: 4. Max electrons: 8.
