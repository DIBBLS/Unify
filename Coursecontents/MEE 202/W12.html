Topic 12.1Eutectic Binary Systems

These binary systems, with unlimited liquid state miscibility and low or negligible solid state miscibility, are referred to as eutectic.

Eutectic reaction: A transition between liquid and mixture of two solid phases, α + β, at eutectic concentration CE. The reaction is written: Liquid → α + β

The melting point of the eutectic alloy is lower than that of the components (eutectic = easy to melt in Greek). At most two phases can be in equilibrium within a phase field. Three phases (L, α, β) may be in equilibrium only at a few points along the eutectic isotherm. Single-phase regions are separated by 2-phase regions.

Eutectic or invariant point: Liquid and two solid phases co-exist in equilibrium at the eutectic composition CE and the eutectic temperature TE. The eutectic isotherm is the horizontal solidus line at TE.

Topic 12.2Invariant Reactions Summary

There are 5 invariant reactions (F = 0). At an invariant point, no independent variables can be changed — temperature, pressure, and composition are all fixed.

#Reaction NameReaction EquationNotes
1EutecticLiquid → α + βLiquid decomposes to TWO solids on cooling
2Eutectoidα → β + γOne solid decomposes to TWO solids. Fe-C: austenite → ferrite + cementite (pearlite)
3PeritecticLiquid + α → βLiquid reacts with solid to form new solid
4Peritectoidα + β → γTwo solids combine to form one solid
5MonotecticL₁ → α + L₂One liquid transforms to solid + different liquid
Fe-C Phase Diagram — Key Points & Phases
Key Fe-C Phases & Transformations
Ferrite (α): BCC iron, max 0.022% C at 727°C. Soft, ductile, magnetic.
Austenite (γ): FCC iron, max 2.11% C at 1148°C. Non-magnetic, forms at high temperature.
Cementite (Fe₃C): Iron carbide, 6.67% C. Very hard and brittle.
Pearlite: Eutectoid mixture of ferrite + cementite (0.77% C, 727°C). Lamellar structure. γ → α + Fe₃C
Ledeburite: Eutectic mixture at 4.30% C, 1148°C. L → γ + Fe₃C

Eutectoid reaction: γ(0.77%C) → α(0.022%C) + Fe₃C(6.67%C)   at 727°C
Eutectic reaction: L(4.30%C) → γ(2.11%C) + Fe₃C(6.67%C)   at 1148°C
★ Why This Matters
The phase diagram question is worth 25 marks in every paper. The lever rule calculation typically gives 6–10 marks. For alloy A-20%B at 550°C: draw the tie line at 550°C, read off the compositions at the liquidus (CL) and solidus (Cα) intersections, then apply the lever rule formulas to get the weight fractions. Always state your tie-line values explicitly before calculating.
⚠ Common Mistake
Students mix up eutectic and eutectoid. Eutectic: involves a LIQUID reacting → two solids. Eutectoid: involves a SOLID reacting → two other solids. In the Fe-C diagram, ledeburite forms at the eutectic point (1148°C, liquid → γ + Fe₃C). Pearlite forms at the eutectoid point (727°C, austenite → ferrite + cementite). The eutectic and eutectoid reactions are similar — both involve decomposition of a single phase into two solid phases. The "-oid" suffix means "resembling" (resembling the eutectic but in solid state).
