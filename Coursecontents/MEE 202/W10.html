Week 10 Cement & Weed as Engineering Materials

Two non-traditional engineering materials that Dr Obasa covers. Cement is one of the most important construction materials ever. Weed (fibrous plants) is an emerging sustainable engineering material. Know the definitions, properties, types, and applications.

Topic 10.1Cement as an Engineering Material

Definition: Cement is a finely ground inorganic substance that, when mixed with water, forms a paste which sets and hardens by chemical reaction (hydration). It binds aggregates like sand and gravel into a solid mass to form concrete or mortar.

Cement is one of the most important and widely used engineering materials in the construction industry. It serves as a key binding material that sets, hardens, and adheres to other materials, making it vital for creating strong and durable structures.

Properties of Cement: Strength (high compressive strength), Workability (workable paste), Durability (resistant to weathering, chemical attack, mechanical wear), Setting Time (initial and final setting times), Heat of Hydration (releases controlled heat, affects large pours).

Sustainability consideration: Although essential, cement production is energy-intensive and a significant source of CO₂ emissions. Modern engineering is shifting towards blended cements (using fly ash, slag), green cement innovations, and recycling and reuse of concrete as aggregate.

Topic 10.2Weed as an Engineering Material

While "weed" typically refers to unwanted plants or vegetation, in engineering and materials science, certain types of fast-growing plants or fibrous weeds are gaining attention for their sustainable, biodegradable, and low-cost properties.

Definition: In this context, "weed" refers to fast-growing, fibrous plants (e.g., water hyacinth, hemp, bamboo grass, cogon grass) that are abundant, invasive (often considered waste), and rich in cellulose, lignin, and natural fibres. These properties make them potential eco-friendly reinforcements or fillers in construction and engineering materials.

Engineering Applications:

a. Natural Fiber Composites: Weeds can be processed into fibers and mixed with polymers (e.g., PLA, epoxy) to produce bio-composites. Used in automotive panels, insulation boards, and interior building panels.

b. Concrete Additives: Ashes or fibers from weeds (e.g., water hyacinth, elephant grass) can be added to cement as pozzolanic materials (for partial cement replacement) or lightweight aggregate/filler. Enhances thermal insulation, reduces density, and supports sustainability.
